\subsection{Quantifiers and Bound Variables}

\begin{principle} \label{princ:analysis-language-as-structure} \hlt{Language Carries Structure}\\
Objects, hypotheses, conclusions, and quantifier order matter. A variable introduced by a quantifier, summation sign, integral, set-builder condition, or similar construction is \hlt{bound} inside that construction; changing its name does not change the statement as long as its scope is unchanged and no free variable is captured. For instance,
\begin{align}
\forall x\in X\ \exists y\in Y\ P(x,y), \qquad
\exists y\in Y\ \forall x\in X\ P(x,y) \nonumber
\end{align}
are different: in the first $y$ may depend on $x$; in the second one $y$ works for all $x$.
\end{principle}
