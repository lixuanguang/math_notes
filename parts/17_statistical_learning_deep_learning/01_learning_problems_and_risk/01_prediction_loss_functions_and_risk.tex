\subsection{Prediction, Loss Functions, and Risk}

A learning problem turns observations into an action rule.  Unlike the inferential problems of Part 15, the primary object is often the future loss of a predictor rather than a confidence statement about a parameter.

\begin{definition} \label{def:sl-prediction-problem-risk} \hlt{Prediction Problem and Risk}\\
Let $(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{A})$ be an input space, $(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{B})$ an output space, and $P$ a probability law on $\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}$.  A \hlt{predictor} is a measurable map $f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{A}_0$ into an action space.  For a loss function $\ell:\mathcal{A}_0\times\mathcal{Y}\to[0,\infty]$, the \hlt{risk} of $f$ is
\[
R(f)=\mathbb{E}_{(X,Y)\sim P}\ell(f(X),Y).
\]
The \hlt{Bayes risk} is $R^*=\inf_fR(f)$, where the infimum ranges over all measurable predictors.
\end{definition}

\begin{example} \label{ex:sl-standard-losses} \hlt{Common Prediction Losses}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
\item Regression commonly uses squared loss $\ell(a,y)=\norm{a-y}_2^2$ or absolute loss $\ell(a,y)=\abs{a-y}$.
\item Binary classification with labels in $\{-1,1\}$ uses zero-one loss $\mathbf{1}_{\{ay\leq0\}}$ for a signed score $a$.
\item Probabilistic classification uses log loss $-\log q_y$ when $q$ is a predicted distribution on labels.
\end{enumerate}
\end{example}

\begin{definition} \label{def:sl-bayes-predictor} \hlt{Bayes Predictors}\\
For squared loss, a Bayes predictor is $f^*(x)=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x]$ whenever the conditional expectation exists.  For absolute loss, any conditional median is Bayes optimal.  For zero-one classification, the Bayes classifier chooses a label with largest conditional probability $P(Y=y\mid X=x)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark} \label{rem:sl-risk-versus-estimation-risk} \hlt{Risk as a Prediction Target}\\
The word ``risk'' also appears in decision theory.  In this part it usually refers to out-of-sample predictive loss under a data-generating law, while Part 15 studies risk as the performance of statistical decisions over a model family.
\end{remark}
