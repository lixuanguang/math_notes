\subsection{Shrinkage, Empirical Bayes, and Regularization}

Risk can often be reduced by intentionally pulling noisy estimates toward a structured target.  The price is bias; the benefit is variance reduction.

\begin{definition} \label{def:shrinkage-estimator} \hlt{Shrinkage Estimator}\\
A \hlt{shrinkage estimator} replaces an unstable estimator $\widehat{\theta}$ by a contracted version
\[
\delta(X)=a(X)\widehat{\theta}+(1-a(X))\theta_{\mathrm{target}},
\qquad 0\leq a(X)\leq1,
\]
where $\theta_{\mathrm{target}}$ encodes a reference value or lower-complexity model.
\end{definition}

\begin{example} \label{ex:ridge-as-shrinkage} \hlt{Ridge Regression as Shrinkage}\\
In linear regression with design matrix $X$ and response $y$, ridge regression solves
\[
\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda}
\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\beta}
\left\{\norm{y-X\beta}_2^2+\lambda\norm{\beta}_2^2\right\}.
\]
When $X^\top X$ is invertible,
\[
\widehat{\beta}_{\lambda}
=(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y,
\]
which shrinks the least-squares solution toward zero along directions with small design information.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:james-stein-phenomenon} \hlt{James--Stein Phenomenon}\\
Let $X\sim\mathcal{N}_d(\theta,\sigma^2I_d)$ with $d\geq3$ and known $\sigma^2$.  The estimator
\[
\delta_{\mathrm{JS}}(X)=
\left(1-\frac{(d-2)\sigma^2}{\norm{X}^2}\right)X
\]
has no larger squared-error risk than $X$ for every $\theta$, and has strictly smaller risk for some $\theta$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition} \label{def:empirical-bayes-rule} \hlt{Empirical Bayes}\\
An \hlt{empirical Bayes} procedure uses data to estimate hyperparameters in a prior family and then applies the corresponding Bayes rule.  In hierarchical notation,
\[
\theta_i\mid\eta\sim\Pi_\eta,
\qquad
X_i\mid\theta_i\sim P_{\theta_i},
\]
the hyperparameter $\eta$ is estimated from the marginal distribution of $X_1,\ldots,X_m$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark} \label{rem:regularization-decision-theory} \hlt{Regularization as Decision-Theoretic Structure}\\
Penalized estimators, shrinkage rules, and empirical Bayes estimators all express a preference for simpler or pooled explanations.  Their statistical value is measured by risk, not by the absence of bias.
\end{remark}
