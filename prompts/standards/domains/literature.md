# Literature Domain Overlay

Use this overlay for close reading, literary interpretation, poetics, narrative analysis, textual criticism, comparative literature, reception, and historically situated literary study.

## Text and location

- Identify the work, author or attributed creator, title, language, edition/version, editor, translator, publisher/date, and stable locator needed to recover the passage. State whether citations use page, line, stanza, act/scene, chapter, folio, or timestamp.
- Distinguish author, narrator, speaker, persona, character, editor, translator, and critic. Do not attribute a narrator's or character's statement directly to the author without an argument.
- Quote exactly from the named witness or edition. Mark omissions, interpolations, emphasis, modernization, normalization, and translation; keep local citations close enough to recover every quotation and paraphrase.

## Analysis and interpretation

- Anchor close reading in an observable textual feature—diction, syntax, image, figure, sound, rhythm, meter, form, genre, narrative order, focalization, typography, or intertext—and show the inference from feature to interpretation.
- Distinguish plot/content summary, formal description, interpretation, historical claim, biographical claim, reception evidence, and critical position. A theme label or isolated quotation is not an argument.
- State the interpretation's scope and degree: one passage, pattern, character, work, corpus, genre, or period. Address materially plausible counterevidence or alternative readings without pretending that all interpretations are equally supported.
- Do not infer authorial intention, reader response, historical influence, or social effect from textual features alone. Support those claims with the relevant archival, reception, or empirical evidence.

## Editions, variants, and translations

- When variants matter, identify witnesses, base text, lemma/reading, editorial intervention, and the method used to select or display readings. Keep attested readings distinct from conjectural emendations.
- Treat translations as versions with named translators and dates. When wording drives the analysis, give the original where feasible and explain the translation choice or compare relevant versions.
- For digital editions or corpora, record release/version, transcription and markup policy, OCR/correction status, search/query method, and access handle. Load history for archival context and linguistics for substantive language analysis.
