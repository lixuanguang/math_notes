# Philosophy Domain Overlay

Use this overlay for conceptual analysis, argument reconstruction, objections and replies, thought experiments, normative reasoning, interpretation of philosophical texts, and balanced reference surveys. Shared modules still own formal mathematical proof, sources, and rendered layout.

## Argument ownership

- Classify each block as a definition, distinction, thesis, argument, reconstruction, objection, reply, counterexample, thought experiment, textual interpretation, or literature survey. State whose position it is and distinguish exposition from endorsement.
- Reconstruct an argument with its conclusion, explicit premises, necessary suppressed premises, and inference relation. Mark additions as reconstruction rather than silently attributing them to the source.
- Distinguish validity from soundness; deductive, inductive, abductive, analogical, and transcendental support; and necessary from sufficient conditions. Do not call an inference formally valid without a checkable form or proof.
- An objection targets an exact premise, inference, definition, scope claim, or consequence and gives a reason. Present the strongest source-supported version before replying; do not treat mere disagreement, rhetorical questions, or remote possibilities as decisive objections.

## Text, concepts, and evaluation

- Define terms of art in plain or previously established language and track materially different senses across authors, traditions, translations, and periods. Do not force contested concepts into one synthetic definition.
- For textual interpretation, cite the exact edition/translation and passage; distinguish quotation, paraphrase, exegetical inference, historical context, and the note author's evaluation. Apply interpretive charity without repairing a source beyond what the text and scholarship support.
- A thought experiment states the scenario, background assumptions, target intuition or principle, and claimed consequence. Record alternative descriptions, intuition disagreement, and empirical assumptions where they matter; an intuition is not automatically evidence shared by all readers.
- For normative claims, state the principle, scope, agents or institutions, affected interests, factual premises, trade-offs, counterexamples, and whether the conclusion is all-things-considered or pro tanto. Source empirical premises from their relevant domains.

## Reference-note standard

- Organize surveys by problems, distinctions, and arguments rather than a chronology of names unless the historical development is itself the subject.
- Represent major source-supported positions and live objections in proportion to the literature. Mark controversial, minority, superseded, or unresolved views; avoid editorializing in the reference voice.
- A philosophical argument is not a repository theorem merely because it has numbered premises. Use theorem-style authority only for a genuinely formal result with proof; load the math overlay for that owner.
