# Fine Arts Domain Overlay

Use this overlay primarily for visual art, architecture, design, craft, photography, moving-image art, performance art documentation, conservation evidence, and art-historical analysis. Music has its own overlay.

## Object and record identity

- Record object/work type, title or names, creator/attribution and role, creation date or range and basis, culture/place, materials/techniques/support, dimensions and orientation, inscriptions/marks, edition/state, current location, repository/accession identifier, and cited source where relevant.
- Preserve attribution and dating uncertainty with terms such as attributed to, workshop/studio of, circle of, after, formerly attributed, circa, before/after, or date range. Do not turn a catalogue's controlled value into greater certainty than its note supports.
- Distinguish the physical work, version/state, installation, performance/event, documentation, conservation state, and digital reproduction. A reproduction is evidence about the work under capture/processing conditions, not the work itself.

## Observation, provenance, and interpretation

- Classify each claim as direct observation, institutional catalogue record, provenance record, archival evidence, conservation/technical examination, scholarly attribution, iconographic identification, formal analysis, contextual interpretation, or reception claim.
- Describe observable form before interpretation: composition, scale, space, line, color/light, surface, material, technique, viewpoint, sequence, sound/movement, and installation conditions. Then make the inference and identify alternative readings or missing evidence.
- For provenance, distinguish ownership, custody, exhibition, sale, transfer, and location; record dates, agents, source documents, gaps, disputed claims, and legal/ethical status only to the degree supported.
- Use controlled Getty vocabularies where helpful while preserving creators' and communities' preferred names, historical terminology, language variants, and contested classifications.

## Images, rights, and technical evidence

- Every image records work identity, view/detail, photographer or source, repository, identifier, date, licence/credit, and material alterations. State crop, rotation, stitching, scale change, color correction, contrast, restoration visualization, or annotation that can affect interpretation.
- Include scale/orientation/color reference when evidentially relevant; do not rely on color alone and provide concise text alternatives. Keep labels and analytical overlays visually distinct from the reproduced object.
- Conservation or scientific claims state sampling location, instrument/method, calibration, imaging conditions, processing, uncertainty, and whether the conclusion is observed, inferred, or provisional.
- Apply copyright, licence, privacy, cultural-property, and community-access restrictions case by case. The CAA fair-use code informs professional reasoning but is not a legal determination; do not claim that image use is lawful merely because it is scholarly.
- Load history for archival chronology, literature for textual works, materials/chemistry for technical analysis, and computer science for digital imaging or collection data.
