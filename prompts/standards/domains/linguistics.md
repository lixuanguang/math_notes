# Linguistics Domain Overlay

Use this overlay for phonetics, phonology, morphology, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, sociolinguistics, historical linguistics, language documentation, corpus work, and psycholinguistic or field data. Shared modules still own mathematical proof, empirical-study structure, sources, figures, and rendered layout.

## Language, data, and claim status

- Identify the language or variety by community-preferred name plus an ISO 639 identifier when useful; add region, community, register, time period, and speaker/population context when they affect the analysis. A code never substitutes for those distinctions.
- Classify each example as attested, elicited, experimentally collected, corpus-derived, reconstructed, or author-constructed. Record source/corpus, stable identifier, speaker or participant code, location/date, and access restrictions at the resolution supported by the source.
- Distinguish observed form, acceptability or interpretation judgment, descriptive generalization, theoretical analysis, typological tendency, and universal claim. State judgment task/scale, participant population, variation, exclusions, and uncertainty when relevant; do not equate one speaker's judgment with a language-wide fact.

## Examples, glosses, and transcription

- Number linguistic examples and keep source attribution locally retrievable. Preserve original orthography; identify normalization, tokenization, segmentation, reconstruction, supplied material, and editorial correction.
- For interlinear examples, align the object-language line, morpheme segmentation, morpheme-by-morpheme gloss, and idiomatic translation. Apply the Leipzig Glossing Rules consistently, define every nonstandard abbreviation, and distinguish hyphens, periods, equals signs, clitics, and non-overt material by declared convention.
- Distinguish phonemic `/.../`, phonetic `[...]`, orthographic `<...>`, and morphophonemic or reconstructed forms using an explicit local convention. Use official IPA symbols and state transcription breadth, relevant diacritics, normalization/font constraints, and audio/video timestamp when available.
- Translations are analytical choices. Identify translator, literal versus idiomatic status, unresolved ambiguity, and culturally specific terms that should remain untranslated or be explained.

## Analysis and reproducibility

- Define theoretical primitives, representation levels, feature systems, constituency/dependency conventions, semantic types, indices, and scope before use. A tree, feature matrix, rule, or logical form illustrates an analysis but is not independent evidence for it.
- For corpus or computational work, record corpus release, licensing/access, sampling unit, query/annotation scheme and version, preprocessing, exclusions, inter-annotator procedure, model/software version, and uncertainty. Load the computer-science overlay when code, models, or benchmarks are substantive.
- For experimental or fieldwork material, record recruitment/consultant context, task, stimuli, counterbalancing, recording equipment, annotation, consent/ethics status when stated, anonymization, compensation, and community/data-governance restrictions. Never invent approval or consent status or expose protected identities.
