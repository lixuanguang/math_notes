# History Domain Overlay

Use this overlay for archival research, historiography, chronology, historical explanation, source criticism, oral history, material/visual evidence, and historical synthesis.

## Evidence trail

- Identify time, place, actors, institutions, and the calendar/geographic conventions needed to interpret the account. Preserve uncertainty in dates, names, boundaries, and attribution.
- Classify evidence as contemporaneous primary material, later recollection, oral history, artifact/image, quantitative record, edition/transcription/translation, or secondary interpretation. “Primary” describes relation to the question, not truthfulness or neutrality.
- Leave a retrievable archival trail: item/document, creator and recipient when known, date, file/folder, series, collection or record group, repository, identifier, version/scan, page/folio/frame, and access date where needed.
- Record provenance, custody, purpose, audience, genre, production conditions, survival/selection, access restrictions, and alterations. Distinguish original, copy, excerpt, transcription, translation, OCR, and later edition.

## Inference and historiography

- Separate what a source states or depicts from the historian's observation, corroborated fact, inference, interpretation, and causal explanation. A source is evidence that someone produced that representation under particular conditions.
- Test representativeness, bias, silence, forgery/authenticity, translation, and retrospective memory. Seek corroboration and materially contrary evidence; explain when the archive cannot answer the question.
- Do not infer causation from chronology alone. State proposed mechanisms, relevant actors and constraints, alternatives, temporal sequence, counterevidence, and the scale at which the explanation applies.
- Map historiography by question, evidence base, method, and disagreement. Credit distinctive findings and interpretations, including the secondary source that led to a primary reference; do not borrow archival citations without acknowledging that path.

## Quotation, oral history, and responsible language

- Transcribe and quote accurately; mark omissions, supplied words, normalization, translation, and changes to primary or secondary sources. Keep historical terminology when evidentially necessary while explaining context and avoiding unmarked endorsement.
- For oral history, record interviewer, narrator, date/place, recording and transcript relation, consent/release status when stated, editing, repository, access restrictions, and memory limitations. Protect identities and culturally restricted knowledge.
- Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence and contemporary categories from later analytical ones. Avoid anachronism and presentism while still stating justified ethical or analytical distinctions explicitly.
- Load literature for close textual reading, political science/economics for domain mechanisms, and computer science for digital-history methods.
