# Political Science Domain Overlay

Use this overlay for political theory, institutions, public policy, comparative politics, international relations, political behavior, surveys, elections, qualitative cases, and quantitative causal or descriptive work.

## Claim and scope

- Classify claims as descriptive, classificatory, causal, predictive, explanatory, normative, interpretive, or model-implied. State the unit and level of analysis, population, geography/jurisdiction, institutions, and time period.
- Define contested concepts and their operational measures separately. Record legal/institutional text and revision, coding rule, measurement validity, and cross-case comparability; do not treat a proxy as the concept itself.
- A theory states mechanism, actors, preferences or motivations, information, institutions, scope conditions, and observable implications. Separate hypotheses specified in advance from post hoc patterns.

## Quantitative, survey, and comparative evidence

- Record data source/release, population and sampling frame, unit of observation, case inclusion, time window, missingness, transformations, weights, and uncertainty.
- For causal work, state treatment/exposure, outcome, estimand, assignment or identification strategy, identifying assumptions, interference/spillovers, timing, diagnostics, and sensitivity. Distinguish association, model dependence, and causal identification.
- For surveys, disclose sponsor and conductor, field dates, target population, sampling/recruitment mode, sample size, question wording/order, response/disposition treatment, weighting, and uncertainty. Do not report a margin of error as though it covered all design and nonsampling errors.
- For comparative or qualitative work, explain case selection, source access, interview/document protocol, coding or process-tracing procedure, within-case chronology, rival explanations, negative or deviant cases, and the limits of generalization.

## Transparency and ethics

- Preserve production transparency and analytic transparency: connect claims to evidence, procedures, code, data, interview guides, codebooks, or appendices when legally, ethically, and epistemologically possible. State access restrictions and persistent identifiers.
- For research involving people, record consent/ethics status when stated, confidentiality and deception practices, compensation, potential harms, data protection, conflicts, sponsorship, and exceptions to disclosure. Never invent institutional approval or reveal protected information.
- Distinguish constitutional/legal text, official record, party or government claim, media report, survey estimate, scholarly interpretation, and normative judgment. An official source is primary evidence for what an institution stated, not automatic proof that the statement is true.
- Load philosophy for substantive normative argument, history for archival/chronological inference, economics for economic mechanisms, and computer science for computational artifacts.
