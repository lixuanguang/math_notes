\subsection{Matrix Analysis}

Matrix analysis translates the structural, geometric, and spectral theory into matrix classes, decompositions, norms, and perturbation tools. The section first treats trace, determinant, positive semidefinite and positive definite matrices, idempotents, nilpotents, projections, spectral radius, and class-oriented matrix functions; then develops QR, Cholesky, singular value decomposition, compact SVD, polar decomposition, matrix modulus, and the structural consequences of factorization algorithms; then studies spectral and Frobenius norms, singular-value inequalities, low-rank approximation, pseudoinverses, conditioning, and numerical stability; and finally collects advanced tools such as Kronecker products, vectorization, Hadamard products, the Schur product theorem, Bauer--Fike, Gershgorin, Hessenberg reduction, Rayleigh quotients, interlacing, Weyl inequalities, and perturbation intuition.
